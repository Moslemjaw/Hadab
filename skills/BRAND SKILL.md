# HADAB Brand Guide

## What HADAB is

HADAB — هَدَب — is a handmade crochet brand and online shop. The name is Arabic for the delicate threads or fringes hanging from the edge of a piece: small details that seem simple but give something character and make it feel complete.

The brand sells **handmade crochet bags, clothes, and accessories**.

Core belief: **details matter**. Texture, shape, color, small imperfections, and the time behind every stitch each add something of their own.

HADAB started with crochet but isn't only about crochet — it's about pieces that feel personal, become part of someone's everyday life, and let them express their style and mood differently.

## Brand story (source text — paraphrase, don't reuse verbatim in output)

Use this as the canonical reference for facts and meaning. When writing "About" copy or brand storytelling, retell it in fresh wording rather than copying these lines directly:

- The name comes from the Arabic word for the fringe/threads at the edge of a piece.
- The brand's core belief is that small, simple details are what give a piece character.
- Everything is handmade, with attention to texture, shape, color, imperfection, and the time invested in each stitch.
- HADAB began with crochet but is meant to grow beyond it, toward personal, everyday, expressive pieces.

## Voice and tone

- **Warm, intimate, unhurried** — like a handwritten note, not a sales pitch.
- **Detail-focused** — draw attention to texture, stitch work, color choices, small imperfections as features, not flaws.
- **Personal and expressive** — talk about how a piece fits into someone's everyday life, style, or mood, not just its specs.
- **Quietly confident, never salesy** — avoid hype words ("amazing," "must-have," "limited time!!"), exclamation-heavy copy, or aggressive urgency.
- **Simple sentences.** Let the craft speak; don't over-explain.
- When referencing the name/meaning of HADAB in copy (e.g., About page, brand intro), it's natural to mention the Arabic word هَدَب and its meaning — but don't force this into every piece of copy (e.g., a single product description doesn't need the etymology lesson).

Avoid:
- Corporate or techy language ("solutions," "premium quality guaranteed," "engineered for...")
- Fast-fashion urgency ("shop now before it's gone," countdown-timer energy)
- Overpromising or generic superlatives ("the best bag you'll ever own")

## Visual identity

### Logo
The HADAB logo mark is an abstract, symmetrical two-loop motif (evoking stitched/looped thread) in the dark brown primary color, alongside a soft, rounded, lowercase custom wordmark "hadab" — organic, slightly irregular letterforms that echo handmade crochet stitches rather than a rigid geometric font. Wordmark is typically rendered in the dark brown.

### Color palette

**Primary colors**
| Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Cream | `#F2E8DD` | Primary background, base neutral |
| Deep Brown | `#4A382F` | Primary text, logo, wordmark, strong accents |

**Secondary colors**
| Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Blush Pink | `#E7C9C8` | Soft accent, backgrounds, highlights |
| Burgundy | `#610C25` | Rich accent, call-to-action or emphasis moments |
| Sage Green | `#7A8060` | Earthy accent, complements the warm neutrals |

Design guidance:
- Cream is the default canvas; deep brown carries the weight of text and branding.
- Use the secondary colors sparingly, as accents (e.g., a highlight, a button, a category tag) — they should feel like intentional pops against the cream/brown base, echoing the "small details matter" philosophy, not compete with it.
- Avoid pure black or pure white in final designs where a warmer neutral (cream/deep brown) can do the job instead — it keeps the handmade, warm feel.
- Any imagery should favor natural light, warm tones, and close-up textures (yarn, stitches, hands at work) over slick studio-product-shot aesthetics.

### Typography feel
No official typeface is specified yet beyond the logo's custom rounded wordmark. When choosing body/UI fonts, favor warm, slightly rounded, humanist typefaces over sharp geometric or techy fonts — something that feels handcrafted and approachable, similar in spirit to the wordmark.

## Applying this to different tasks

- **Product descriptions**: Lead with a sensory or personal detail (texture, color story, how it feels to wear/carry), not a spec list. Keep it short and warm.
- **Website/UI copy**: Simple, personal phrasing over corporate e-commerce boilerplate ("Add to bag" over "Add to Cart — Buy Now!", etc., when a warmer alternative exists).
- **Social captions**: Can be more casual/personal, still detail-focused; avoid emoji-heavy or hype-driven caption styles.
- **Visual/UI design work**: Use the color table above exactly (hex values), cream-forward layouts, and the brand's rounded/organic visual language described above.
- **Packaging/print**: Same palette and voice; let whitespace and warm neutrals breathe rather than filling every inch.

If a task calls for something the story/palette doesn't cover (e.g., a brand-new product category, a seasonal campaign tone), extend the existing voice and palette rather than introducing a clashing style, and flag the gap to the user rather than guessing silently.
